## The idea, in one paragraph

Existing radio SETI surveys, including SETI@home and Breakthrough Listen, produced large datasets but no confirmed technosignatures. Modern AI pattern-recognition methods have advanced considerably since much of that data was first analyzed. The proposal is a carefully scoped reanalysis of publicly available or previously processed radio data using present-day anomaly-detection and clustering techniques, under an explicit "trust but verify" protocol that requires synthetic-signal injection, independent confirmation where possible, and human review of any high-ranking candidates. The first, falsifiable version of that question is whether an anomaly-detection stage can recover narrowband signals whose drift is not constant, a class earlier pipelines were not built to catch. The evidence for that claim is thin, so it is a pilot test with go/kill criteria, not a premise. The goal is not to claim a discovery, but to measure the answer, document it either way, and leave behind benchmark data that future surveys can use.

RE-SETI is an independent proposal. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by UC Berkeley or its SETI@home project, Breakthrough Listen, or the SETI Institute.
