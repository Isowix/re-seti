# Funding and Partnerships

*RE-SETI proposal archive v1.2 · 23 November 2026 · Prepared by Phil Rail*

> **Status.** No funding has been sought or raised, and no partnerships exist. This document lists *possible* avenues to explore after review. Naming an organization here does not imply it has been contacted or has shown interest.

## Why the model can be lean

The SETI@home team has said the biggest obstacle to a new volunteer-computing SETI project is personnel, "and personnel means salaries" [1]. This proposal aims to keep the core team small by automating routine operations and by renting computing instead of owning hardware (documents 04 and 05).

Computing itself is the cheap part. The largest non-staff cost is expected to be **moving data**: at published cloud egress prices [6], sending all ~31 PB held by Breakthrough Listen [5] would cost on the order of US$1.5 million or more, while a reduced subset the size of one major survey's filterbank products (142 TB [5]) would be on the order of US$10,000, if egress fees apply at all (document 05, section 5). The project therefore plans to reduce data near the archive and move only what it needs. A real budget will be set from the measured numbers of the Phase 0 single-GPU pilot (document 02), which itself needs only one consumer GPU and public data.

## Possible funding avenues

### Private philanthropy

SETI research has historically depended heavily on private money. Two examples show the scale that donors have committed:

- **Breakthrough Listen**: announced in July 2015 as a 10-year, US$100 million commitment from the Breakthrough Prize Foundation [2].
- **SETI Institute**: received a US$200 million bequest from the estate of Qualcomm co-founder Franklin Antonio, announced in November 2023 [3]. The SETI Institute noted at the time that SETI research is eligible for only limited federal funding and otherwise depends on philanthropy [3]. It has since used the bequest to fund internal research and education grants (the STRIDE program) [4].

These are precedents for donor interest in the field, not sources available to this project. Both organizations fund their own programs; any approach would be a proposal for collaboration, made only after review.

### Public science grants

Government research councils fund astronomy, data science and citizen science. Eligibility usually requires a host institution, such as a university or registered non-profit, which this project does not have. A university partner would likely be needed before applying.

### Observatory and research partnerships

Collaboration with groups that hold data or operate telescopes would add scientific credibility and is essential for follow-up re-observation. Potential conversations, after review, include Breakthrough Listen and Berkeley SETI Research Center (data and methods), and teams operating instruments such as FAST, the Green Bank Telescope, MeerKAT or the Allen Telescope Array (re-observation requests). The SETI@home team's own published lessons [1] would be a natural starting point for any approach to UC Berkeley.

### Open-source sponsorship

Open tools and datasets can attract support through programs such as GitHub Sponsors or fiscal sponsorship by open-source foundations. These typically fund maintenance at a modest scale rather than research.

### Volunteer and in-kind contributions

The BOINC volunteer community contributes computing time rather than money. A volunteer network would be used only if the pilot shows it is needed and the data can be delivered affordably (document 02). Cloud providers sometimes offer research credits; these would be welcome but must not create dependence on a single provider.

## Principles

- No funding will be accepted that compromises the "trust but verify" rules or the publication of null results.
- Funders will not have a say in which results are published.
- Any funding received will be reported publicly.
